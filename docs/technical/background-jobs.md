# Background jobs contract

Jobs run in process with distributed coordination. This is the complete required job inventory; it is not a list of accepted or fully implemented jobs.

| Name | Cadence | Lease TTL | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `sla-scan` | 5 min | 5 min | Recomputes SLA state for **open** work items from `sla_started_at`; emits `sla.at_risk` / `sla.breached` on edges via `work_item_sla_cache`. It does **not** emit `sla.met` / `sla.missed` — a just-completed item has `resolved_at` set and is outside the candidate set below, so those two are emitted by the `WF-17` transition into a `completed`-group state ([events.md](events.md)) |
| `reminder-scan` | 15 min | 5 min | `work_item.due_soon` / `overdue`, `prerequisite.overdue`, `approval.expiring` / `expired` (writes `reminder_50_sent_at` / `reminder_90_sent_at` so nothing repeats), walks escalation paths (`NO-22`), auto-declines submissions in `clarifying` past the window (`IQ-15`), flags `sla_pause` rows open > 30 days, flags KB articles past `review_due_at` |
| `scheduled-transition` | 1 min | 1 min | Fires due `scheduled_transition` rows (`state = 'pending'`, `due_at <= now()`) as the already-resolved concrete hop `from_state_id → to_state_id` through the system-actor transition path (`WF-19`), claiming each row (`fired`) inside the transition's own transaction; a row whose item has left `from_state_id`, or is deleted/archived, is marked `cancelled`. The stored hop needs no edge in the current workflow version; if one exists for the same template pair its guards and approval/CAB gate are honoured. A hop that can never apply (target state gone, guard or approval gate refusing, SLA pause conflict) is `cancelled` with a logged reason and an internal `scheduled_transition_cancelled` activity row (the table has no reason column); only an unexpected error leaves the row `pending` for the next tick. The `schedule_transition` transaction also cancels pending rows when the item leaves `from_state_id`, and move/archive/delete cancel them. Moved out of `reminder-scan`'s 15-minute cadence so "pending until" fires within a minute |
| `outbox-drain` | 30 s | **none — `SKIP LOCKED`** | Processes parent event envelopes and independently claims immediate notification children or sealed digest groups; applies retry/backoff and current reach checks; handles webhook deliveries and auto-disables a webhook failing for 24 h, emitting `webhook.auto_disabled`. Runs on every replica concurrently by design |
| `customer-invitation-delivery` | 1 min | Per-row fenced 40 s | Sends at most five queued customer invitation emails per run, with a 5 s provider deadline and three attempts. Ciphertext expires after at most 15 min and is erased on send, invalidation or cleanup. Cleanup runs before provider checks and at startup, including when SMTP is disabled. Manual runs are refused. Worker downtime can delay physical erasure; expired ciphertext is never decrypted/sent and its backlog degrades health. SMTP acknowledgement followed by a crash can cause a duplicate email; acceptance remains single-use. |
| `notification-digest` | hourly | 5 min | Seals collecting digest groups whose stored UTC windows have ended; it does not call a provider. `outbox-drain` also seals a due group atomically when claiming it, so a delayed sweep cannot block delivery |
| `quiet-hours-release` | 1 min | 15 min (job lease) + `SKIP LOCKED` row claim | Delivers eligible legacy-path notifications using `quietHoursHeldUntil` in `notification.event_data` as a durable queue marker: producers write it due immediately in the inbox transaction, and quiet-hours evaluation may move it to a later deadline. Claims 20 rows per tick, sequentially; a taking replica pushes each row's marker 20 min ahead (longer than the 15 min job lease and the worst-case batch of about 10 min of slow endpoints, so no run re-claims a row another is still delivering). A crash before send retries after the claim lease; a crash after send but before marker clear may re-send, so delivery is **at-least-once**. Provider failures retain the marker for retry; terminal ineligible/unreachable/unconfigured deliveries clear it. Re-checks quiet hours before sending; abandons markers older than 7 days. SLA breach and approval expiry are exempt |
| `cycle-snapshot` | daily at 00:15 UTC | 15 min | `CY-9`: snapshots cycles completed by date as well as explicit completion, idempotently under the job lease. Daily cadence is required; the UTC time and lease are operational choices. Velocity uses completed-cycle snapshots rather than extrapolating from an incomplete sample |
| `metrics-snapshot` | hourly | 15 min | `RP-9`: writes `metric_snapshot` at **day grain only** (no hourly grain, no 00:15 rollup, nothing for the current day) for every workspace with `feature.reports` on: SLA outcomes (resolution and first response) and throughput, as counts per project, day, priority and type, plus a `metric_snapshot_day` marker per workspace and day so a day with nothing to count is told apart from a day never computed. Each run recomputes the last three closed days and fills missing days newest first (120 per workspace per run) back to 800 days (the contract names no retention; 800 covers the widest window plus its comparison period); a pruned or missing day is read live, so retention never changes a number. A day is replaced in one transaction under an advisory lock, so a rerun leaves identical rows. The rows are aggregates with no person, rate or cost data: **legal holds do not apply**. `cycle_snapshot` is not written by this job |
| `search-reindex` | 10 min | 10 min | Catches up rows whose search vector is stale |
| `audit-purge` | daily 03:00 | 30 min | Deletes `audit_log` rows past retention as `taskdesk_maint`; **skips rows whose `organisation_id` or actor is under an open `legal_hold`** (join `legal_hold` on `lifted_at is null`); writes an `audit_chain_anchor` row **before** deleting, and does not delete if the anchor cannot be written; writes its own audit row |
| `session-cleanup` | daily 03:15 | 5 min | Physically deletes expired sessions, invitations, idempotency keys, up to 1,000 expired shared SCIM ingress counters per run (hold-exempt ephemeral security state; active budgets are preserved), expired `notification_preference_handoff` rows, and expired `outbox_dedupe_reservation` rows; purges eligible read notifications, terminal notification children, empty terminal digest groups, and parent event envelopes in child-before-parent order under the retention and legal-hold rules below; and purges soft-deleted rows past their window — **the soft-delete purge skips any row whose organisation or person is under an open `legal_hold`**, and leaves it soft-deleted until the hold lifts. Reservation lease validity and takeover are enforced at `lease_expires_at`; they do not wait for this daily physical cleanup. |
| `custom-field-purge` | daily 03:45 | 30 min | `CF-8`: hard-deletes a `custom_field` soft-deleted more than 30 days ago (the same window `POST /api/custom-fields/{id}/restore` enforces) with its `custom_field_value` and `custom_field_type_visibility` rows (cascade), one `custom_field.purged` audit row per field (key and value count only). **Skips a field while an open `legal_hold` covers its workspace's organisation, any value's organisation or project, or a person attached to a work item holding a value**; re-checks eligibility and holds under a row lock per field |
| `page-purge` | daily 03:50 | 30 min | `PG-7`: hard-deletes a `project_page` whose restore window has passed (`purge_after <= now()`, delete time + 30 days; the same column `POST /api/projects/{projectId}/pages/{id}/undelete` enforces) with its `project_page_version` rows (cascade), one `page.purged` audit row per page (title and version count only). **Skips a page while an open `legal_hold` covers its workspace's or project's organisation, or a person attached to the page or its versions**, and while it still has a child page in any state (leaf-first; `project_page_parent_fk` is NO ACTION); re-checks eligibility and holds under a row lock per page |
| `attachment-gc` | daily 03:30 | 30 min | Removes objects for `attachment.state = 'deleted'` rows and orphans; **skips attachments whose `organisation_id` is under an open `legal_hold`** — which is why `attachment.workspace_id` / `organisation_id` are stored on the row ([data-model.md](data-model.md)) |
| `attachment-pending-cleanup` | hourly | 5 min | Deletes `attachment` rows still `pending` after an hour (presign never completed) |
| `timer-sweeper` | 15 min | 5 min | Stops `running_timer` rows older than 12 h, writing a capped `time_entry` |
| `plugin-health` | 10 min | 2 min | Pings configured plugins **and each enabled `identity_connection`'s OIDC discovery document** (`IP-25`); surfaces failures in God Mode → Health |
| `backup-check` | hourly | 2 min | Raises the Health warning when no `backup_run` succeeded in 48 h |
| `position-rebalance` | on demand | 5 min | Triggered when a rank write leaves the gap between two neighbouring `work_item.position` (`numeric(20,10)`) values below `1e-6`; renumbers the whole affected state (or backlog) partition to evenly-spaced values in one transaction (`WI-12`) |
| `import-run` | on demand | 1 h, per-run lease `import-run:<run id>` | Executes a dry-run-approved engine import (`import_run.source_connection_id` set), one transaction per chunk on the **bulk write path**, as the run's starter (`started_by` / `started_by_api_key_id`, never the system actor). Per chunk it re-checks live instance-admin authority, key enabled state and capabilities, and project reach, and **pauses** the run (`paused_reason`) when any is lost, the mapping or approval binding changed, the approved count would be exceeded, or the source fails. The cursor, stats and loss rows commit with the chunk, so a pause, crash or restart resumes after the last committed chunk. Started by `POST /api/imports/{id}/execute` and `/resume` (resume also re-kicks a run left `running` after a restart); nothing fires the registered sweep handler on a clock, so there is no automatic restart recovery. The lease is not renewed mid-run (no renew primitive exists); a second worker is fenced by a compare-and-set on the stored cursor |
| `import-preview-cleanup` | 1 min | 1 min | Redacts expired import staging payloads and per-source report rows in batches of 100 with run-before-chunk locks. Pending approvals remain fenced by their own lifecycle; execution rejects expired bindings synchronously. |
| `report-export` | on demand + 1 min poll | 30 min | `RP-10`: renders a queued `report_export` (a screen run that was truncated or above 150 rows) into stored CSV under the requester's reach, re-resolving the requester and ANDing their current rights with the ones they had when asking (a revoked `report:export`, membership or feature fails the export; `time_entry:manage_rates` is re-checked again at download). Claims rows with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, five per run; started right after an export is queued and polled every minute so a restart loses nothing. At most three queued or running exports per person (checked under an advisory lock). Ready content expires after 24 h (`expired`, content dropped), a run abandoned for 45 min becomes `failed`, finished rows are deleted after 14 days. The requester polls `GET /api/report-exports/{id}` and downloads from the authenticated `GET /api/report-exports/{id}/download` (audited `report.exported`); **there is no email step** (the link in the original contract is not built). API-key and impersonation requests are never queued: they get the synchronous CSV with its truncation stated |
| `secrets-rekey` | on demand | 30 min | Re-encrypts every `instance_plugin_config.secrets`, `identity_connection.client_secret`, **webhook signing secret (`webhook.secret`, `webhook.secret_previous`) and notification token (`user_notification_preference.ntfy_token`, `gotify_token`, `webhook_secret`; `enc:v2:` base64url of the same envelope, legacy `enc:v1:` read and rewritten)** from `TASKDESK_ENCRYPTION_KEY_PREVIOUS` to the current key, writing `key_id` per row — see the runbook |
| `automation-schedule` | 1 min | 1 min, **per rule** | Evaluates every enabled `automation` whose `trigger = 'schedule'` and whose `schedule_cron` is due against the rule's `project_filter`, then invokes it. Rule crons are stored rows, not `croner` registrations: the job wakes each minute, selects the due rules and runs them, so a rule edited in the UI takes effect on the next tick with no scheduler reload. The lease name is **one per rule** — `automation:<automation_id>` — so 40 replicas run each rule once and a slow rule does not block the others. This is the runner behind the `schedule` trigger that [events.md](events.md) calls "not an event" |
| `pending-action-expire` | 1 min | 1 min | Marks `pending_action` rows past `expires_at` as `expired` and emits `pending_action.decided` (`PA-8`); invalidates pending rows whose requester was deactivated or whose credential was revoked since (`PA-9`) |

`customer-invitation-delivery` is the exception to the jobs-role scheduler: `web` and `all` run its one-minute poller because invitation decryption keys live only in that process, are owned by its boot UUID, expire within 15 minutes, and are zeroed on shutdown. `jobs` never claims invitation deliveries. Other registered jobs and the manual-run worker belong to `jobs` and `all`; every role still exposes the complete registered catalog and persisted configuration to God Mode.

## Coordination and retention

Lease ownership is hostname + PID + boot ID. Acquire, renew and release by exact owner, use database time and stop work on lost lease. Per-rule automation leases prevent one slow rule blocking others. Outbox workers use independent `SKIP LOCKED` claims rather than a global lease; every retry revalidates eligibility and reach.

God Mode job cadence/enable/manual-run changes require `instance:manage_jobs` and audit. Retention obeys person, source, recipient and resource-owner legal holds; unresolved ownership retains history conservatively. Purge eligible children before empty digest groups and parent envelopes. Pending deliveries and unread inbox entries are not terminal retention candidates. Coordination-reservation expiry is separate from history retention. Attachment GC respects recovery time and legal holds.

`audit-verify` is an on-demand read-only CLI/administration action and restore-drill check, not a scheduled job. It verifies the chain from its retained anchor and reports the first break.

## Current scheduler mapping

The scheduler currently registers `outbox-drain`, `quiet-hours-release`, `notification-digest` (seals ended digest groups only; no digest sender exists yet, and no external channel delivers a digest-eligible event), `attachment-pending-cleanup`, `attachment-gc`, `sla-scan`, `session-cleanup` (also purges read notifications past `notification_retention_days`, hold-aware), `custom-field-purge`, `page-purge`, `pending-action-expire`, `reminder-scan` (approval expiry/reminders, the `NO-22` SLA escalation walk over `sla_escalation`, and the `IQ-15` clarification auto-decline; the other contract steps are not built), `scheduled-transition`, and the inherited `due-date-reminders` compatibility job. `due-date-reminders` runs every five minutes; it is not an additional target job and does not prove the complete reminder-scan contract. Position rebalance has a separate on-demand implementation. Registration alone does not prove the required job behavior or configurable administration.

The outbox consumer registry is no longer empty: `outbox-drain` registers the `webhook` and `automation` parent consumers on its first run. They only enqueue `webhook_delivery` and `automation_run` rows inside the claim transaction; `outbox-drain` then drains those queues in a second step (`drainP4Consumers`: signed webhook POSTs with retry and the 24 h auto-disable, then automation runs under per-rule leases). Neither step has run against Postgres yet.

`secrets-rekey` has its job body (`scheduler/secrets-rekey.ts`, resumable by key id) and Health reports completion. It is registered as an on-demand (paused, never clock-fired) job and started by the elevated `POST /api/instance/plugins/rotate-secrets` (PA-15 operation `secrets_rotate`), which returns `202` and runs the leased job in the background. `impersonation-cap` runs every minute and ends impersonation sessions that reached their 30-minute cap without an exit (`GM-7`), reading the audit trail so it works after session cleanup.
