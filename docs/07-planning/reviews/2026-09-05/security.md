## Strengths

- **The route policy registry now enumerates Hono's router, not the OpenAPI document.** `rbac.md`
  policy kind 5 (`delegated: 'better-auth' | 'websocket' | 'metrics'`) exists precisely because
  "the OpenAPI document does not know about `/auth/*`, `/ws` or `/metrics`, and those are precisely
  the surfaces v1 leaked through". This closes the single largest structural hole an OpenAPI-derived
  coverage test would have had, and it is the difference between a real control and a plausible one.
- **Five closed policy kinds, type-bound to the route table.** `PolicyMap<typeof routes>` makes a
  path/policy mismatch a *type* error, `orOwner` expresses ownership without an ad-hoc OR, and
  `{ authenticated: true, self: true }` / `{ portal, predicate }` replace the vague `(self)` and
  `(portal session)` annotations the feature specs previously carried.
- **Reach and authority are separate evaluator arguments**, `sees_all` grants reach only, and the
  permission-matrix test runs twice — once for capability, once for reach — plus a custom-role pass.
  Testing reach separately is what catches the v1 shape of bug.
- **404-not-403 is consistent** across `rbac.md`, `multi-tenancy.md` and `api-design.md`, and now
  extends *within* a customer organisation: `customer_visibility = 'private'` is 404 to a colleague,
  "exactly like a cross-organisation record".
- **Identity resolved from the database on every request, never from a token claim**, with a stated
  30-second worst-case revocation budget and explicit cache invalidation on membership/role change.
  better-auth's organisation plugin is deliberately *off* so the directory is ours.
- **Portal boundary at three points** — OIDC callback, per-request host-vs-`session.portal` comparison
  (a real column, not an inference), and a build-time bundle-graph assertion — with the corpus
  explicitly stating the bundle split "is **not** the security boundary. The server is."
- **Customer constraints are behavioural, in `packages/domain`, enforced regardless of capabilities**,
  and the customer role's capability list ends "and nothing else, ever".
- **Impersonation is properly bounded** (GM-7…GM-11): elevated to start, 30-minute cap, per-request
  audit rows carrying `impersonator_id`, no approvals, no elevated actions, no nested impersonation,
  no credential/MFA change, no export, never another instance admin, always-available exit route.
- **Encryption-key rotation is operator-staged and resumable** — `TASKDESK_ENCRYPTION_KEY_PREVIOUS`,
  per-row `key_id`, a resumable `secrets-rekey` job, Health reporting completion — rather than one
  long transaction.
- **Attachment handling is strong for a plan**: generated object keys, magic-byte sniffing at
  `complete`, allowlist-not-denylist, SVG rejected by default, 5-minute presigned downloads after a
  policy check, a dedicated `files.<domain>` origin, `customer_visible` filtered server-side, and
  "an attachment on an internal comment is always internal, whatever its own flag says".
- **The WebSocket carries only "what changed"**, forcing clients back through the policy-enforced
  REST path so the socket never becomes a second, unaudited data path.
- **Outbox rows written in the same transaction as the domain change**, with visible delivery history,
  dead-lettering, and auto-disable of an endpoint failing for 24 hours.
- **Audit is treated as a control, not a log**: `actor_type` (`person|automation|system|api_key`),
  `api_key_id`, `impersonator_id`, `actor_ip`, `trace_id`, append-only "enforced by database grants",
  `audit-purge` running as a separate `taskdesk_maint` role, and the purge auditing itself.
- **Idempotency keys are scoped `(actor_id, key)`** with an in-flight duplicate returning 409.
- **Branding `css_overrides` is bounded to an enumerated variable list and rejected server-side** —
  a CSS-injection surface closed before it was opened.
- **Supply chain has a real baseline**: cosign keyless signing plus a build-provenance attestation,
  `scripts/deploy.sh` verifying the signature before starting a new digest, SBOM per release, Trivy
  gate, `latest` meaning latest *stable*, and the kaneo snapshot itself named as a supply-chain input
  to be scanned and SHA-pinned *before* the fork commit.
- **Marketplace metering is an opt-in `license` plugin with `license.none` as default** (ADR 0013),
  correctly refusing both a phone-home default and a second build artefact needing separate patching.
- **Backup doctrine is honest**: `.env` named as the third backup artefact, "an untested backup is not
  a backup", a monthly restore drill with a written record, and a restore checklist whose items 5–7
  are exactly what a naive restore test misses.
- **Automations cannot exceed a configured effective role**, that role is capped at the author's own
  rank, rules default to disabled, and a dry-run against recent history precedes enabling.

## Threats not addressed

These are attacker goals and adversary classes the corpus does not model anywhere. Each is
plausible for this product, and each currently has no stated control, detection or acceptance.

1. **A malicious or compromised workspace administrator.** The entire authorization model is
   designed to constrain people *below* the top of a workspace, and the guardrails are all
   about not exceeding your own rank. Nothing addresses an `owner` or `admin` acting
   maliciously *within* their authority: minting a workspace service key with capabilities
   they choose, creating a workspace-wide webhook that streams every event to an external
   endpoint, writing an automation that posts internal data into public comments, granting
   `sees_all`, or deleting a project. There is no separation of duties, no four-eyes
   requirement for any workspace-level action, no anomaly detection ("this account just
   created twelve service keys"), and no notification to anyone else when authority changes.
   The audit log records it — after the fact, and only to whoever chooses to look.

2. **A hostile customer organisation.** Customers are modelled as a *constrained* actor, never
   as an *adversarial* one. Their capabilities are correctly minimal, but the attack surface
   they legitimately reach is not analysed: uploading malware to staff (no scanning);
   prompt-injecting the AI classification/summarisation features and any staff MCP agent
   through ticket text; exhausting unlimited storage and unlimited portal-user quotas;
   abusing `escalate priority`, `reopen` and `withdraw` in loops; probing tenancy through
   filter-and-count inference; and using the portal's rate-limit classes (60 writes/min/person
   against an unlimited number of invited portal users) as a distributed write channel.

3. **A compromised plugin credential.** `instance_plugin_config.secrets` holds OIDC client
   secrets, SMTP credentials, S3 and Azure keys, AI provider keys and Slack/Teams webhook URLs.
   The corpus covers *storage* of these thoroughly and covers *nothing* about what happens when
   one leaks: no per-plugin rotation runbook (only whole-key rotation), no detection of a
   credential being used from elsewhere, no blast-radius statement (an S3 key typically grants
   access to every tenant's attachments; an OIDC client secret plus the redirect URI is an
   account-takeover primitive), and no guidance to scope those credentials narrowly when
   configuring them.

4. **An insider with database access.** An operator, DBA, or anyone holding
   `TASKDESK_DATABASE_URL` — which by design also appears in `.env`, in backups, and in the
   documented break-glass path — can mint an instance administrator, read every session row,
   read every audit row, and (absent the grants that only `audit-trail.md` promises) rewrite
   history. With `TASKDESK_ENCRYPTION_KEY` as well, they hold every plugin secret. The corpus
   never states this as a threat model boundary, never proposes separation (an application role
   distinct from a migration role distinct from a maintenance role, beyond the single
   `taskdesk_maint` mention), and never proposes tamper-evidence that would survive it.

5. **A compromised kaneo upstream before the fork.** The control is "its lockfile is audited and
   its dependency tree scanned **before** the fork commit, and the commit SHA taken is recorded".
   That detects *known vulnerabilities*. It does not detect a *deliberately planted* backdoor —
   in kaneo's own source, in a kaneo maintainer's npm account, or in a transitive dependency
   published shortly before the snapshot. Nothing in the corpus calls for a human review of the
   inherited code (as opposed to a scanner pass), a check of kaneo's release-signing or
   maintainer history, or a reproducible-build comparison. Given that P0 is "copy kaneo… rebrand
   and gate", the entire product's trust base is this one snapshot.

6. **A compromised CI identity.** Images are signed "cosign, keyless via **the CI OIDC identity**"
   and `scripts/deploy.sh` verifies that signature. A compromised GitHub Actions workflow, a
   malicious pull-request-triggered job, or a compromised repository secret therefore produces a
   *validly signed* malicious image that every deployment accepts. The corpus has no statement
   about workflow hardening, branch protection on the release workflow, restricted `id-token`
   permissions, or a policy asserting *which* CI identity and *which* workflow may sign.

7. **A malicious MCP client, or a malicious MCP server presented to a user.** `MC-11`–`MC-13`
   distribute `@taskdesk/mcp` via npm with an API key in the client's environment. Not addressed:
   a typosquatted or compromised npm package harvesting `TASKDESK_API_KEY`; a user being
   socially engineered into pointing `TASKDESK_API_URL` at an attacker-controlled host that
   proxies and records; or an MCP client sharing the key across untrusted tool contexts.

8. **Account-recovery social engineering.** `POST /api/instance/users/{id}/reset-mfa` is an
   instance-admin action requiring elevation from the admin — but nothing specifies how the
   *requester's* identity is verified before an admin resets someone's second factor. This is
   the most commonly exploited path into an MFA-protected account and it has no stated procedure.

9. **Encryption at rest below the application.** Plugin secrets are encrypted; nothing else is.
   There is no stated requirement for full-disk or volume encryption on the Postgres data
   directory, the object store, or the backup target — so every work item, comment, attachment,
   audit row and PII field is at rest in plaintext by default. For a product sold to customers
   with DPAs, this is a question that will be asked.

10. **Sub-processor and data-egress inventory.** Sentry receives errors "with the user's
    organisation as a tag"; the AI plugins send ticket content to a third party; the marketplace
    plugin reports usage to AWS; OTLP ships traces. There is no single statement of "what leaves
    this instance, to whom, under which configuration" — which is both a customer-facing
    requirement and the thing an operator needs to answer honestly.

11. **Timing and behavioural side channels on the 404 rule.** The 404-not-403 rule is enforced
    at the status-code level. Nothing addresses whether an out-of-reach 404 is distinguishable
    from a genuine 404 by *latency* (the former may load and discard a row), by error-body
    shape, or by rate-limit accounting — which would reduce the tenancy guarantee to an
    inconvenience for a patient attacker.

12. **Legal hold and e-discovery versus retention.** `audit-purge` deletes on a schedule and
    audits itself; `activity` is retained forever; organisation deletion tombstones audit rows.
    There is no mechanism to *suspend* deletion for a specific organisation or person under
    legal hold, and no export path that would satisfy a discovery request scoped to one tenant.

## Summary

This is an unusually strong security posture for a planning corpus, and the mid-review revisions
materially improved it: the route-coverage test now enumerates Hono's router rather than the
OpenAPI document — closing the largest structural hole a coverage-based control could have — and
the five closed policy kinds, strict rank guardrails, bounded impersonation, resumable `key_id`
rotation, and `(actor_id, key)` idempotency scoping each fix a real defect. The corpus's central
thesis, that v1's eleven holes were failures of *omission* and must therefore be made mechanically
detectable, is correct and is now backed by mechanisms that would plausibly work.

The remaining high-severity gaps cluster in four places. First, **authorization below the route**:
the policy registry proves a check exists on every route, but scope-object resolution is
unspecified, `project:update` can silently grant reach to a whole team via `owner_team_id`, service
API keys are explicitly *not* clamped to their creator's authority, and tenant isolation rests on a
lint rule with no backstop. Second, **identity**: JIT provisioning maps organisation from an email
domain with no `email_verified` requirement and no domain-to-provider binding, and the words CSRF,
PKCE, `state` and `nonce` appear nowhere in the entire documentation tree. Third, **long-lived and
machine channels**: WebSocket subscriptions are authorized once and never re-checked against the
revocation path the rest of the system is proud of, and the AI and MCP surfaces — both of which
consume attacker-authored customer text while holding staff authority — have no security treatment
at all. Fourth, **deployment**: `TASKDESK_TRUST_PROXY` defaults to `true` while the shared-Traefik
pattern leaves the application port reachable, making client IP attacker-controlled and defeating
the auth rate limit, the API-key IP allowlist and the integrity of `audit_log.actor_ip`.

The corpus also has a visible seam at P0: every control is specified for code that does not exist,
while the actual first act is importing kaneo's existing routers — including its public anonymous
project boards, still undecided and mentioned in no security document — into a gate whose exit
criterion is "green on an **empty** application". Retrofitting kaneo's surface into the five policy
kinds is the largest security task in the project and is currently described only as a bullet in a
planning document. Finally, for a product other people will self-host under AGPL, the absence of any
vulnerability-disclosure policy, advisory channel or patch-communication path is a gap in obligation
rather than in engineering, and it should be closed before the first external install, not before
the first external customer.

## Withdrawn after revision

Raised against the pre-revision documents and now genuinely fixed; recorded so the same points are
not re-raised in a later review:

- *Route coverage derived from the OpenAPI document, so `/auth/*`, `/ws` and `/metrics` are
  invisible* — fixed by `rbac.md` policy kind 5 and the explicit statement that the test enumerates
  Hono's router.
- *Rank guardrail permitted editing a peer-ranked role* — fixed by "greater than or equal to your
  own highest rank", plus the prohibition on creating a role at or above your own rank.
- *Custom roles untested by the permission matrix* — fixed by the third CI test in `rbac.md` and
  `custom-role.test.ts` in the testing strategy.
- *Idempotency keys possibly global, allowing cross-tenant response retrieval* — fixed by the
  unique `(actor_id, key)` constraint in `data-model.md`.
- *Encryption-key rotation relying on one long transaction with no key version* — fixed by
  `GM-12`–`GM-14`: `key_id` per row, `TASKDESK_ENCRYPTION_KEY_PREVIOUS`, a resumable job.
- *Impersonation able to create durable authority and read arbitrary tenant data unchecked* —
  substantially fixed by `GM-9`/`GM-10` (no approvals, no elevated actions, no nested impersonation,
  no credential or MFA change, no export, never another instance admin). Two residuals remain and
  are **not** withdrawn: `GM-9` does not exclude creating API keys, webhooks, invitations or role
  edits — durable authority that outlives the 30-minute cap — and the impersonated user is never
  notified.
- *Rate limiting silently degraded without Valkey* — fixed by the explicit statement that counters
  are per-replica and approximate without it and that a multi-replica deployment must configure it.
- *Manual job triggering uncapability-checked and unaudited* — fixed by `instance:manage_jobs`,
  audited, in both `background-jobs.md` and `god-mode.md`.
- *Automations and jobs with no actor attribution* — fixed by `actor_type`
  (`person | automation | system | api_key`) on `audit_log`, `activity` and `comment`.
- *Account linking on email alone* — addressed by the placeholder-claim rule ("linked… **after the
  email is verified**… This is the only path by which a login attaches to a pre-existing directory
